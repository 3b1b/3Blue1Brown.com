The more I think about what makes an LLM useful, either in chat or as an agent, the more I realize how important effective web search is. Pretty much whenever you need factual information, hoping to retrieve that from what's compressed into the weights is not only unreliable, but undesirable from a credit-attribution standpoint.

The most fascinating part of chatting with the Exa team was to hear how different the challenge of search for LLMs is from the challenge of search for humans. You can expect much richer queries, and the desired response is likely not one important top search result, but a fuller set of pages that are most helpful to load into context. This means completely rethinking how you index the web in the first place, and how you structure return results. I especially like Sam Mitchell's framing in our interview that the goal is to create "attention over everything".

Given how lean the team is compared to the scale of what they're dealing with, it would be easy to imagine people feeling daunted. Instead, I saw only excitement from the people I chatted with. And I get it: when you're on a team of highly capable peers, and you're able to take on significant tasks as an individual that would have previously required a full team, it can be incredibly compelling.

On a personal note, I believe that effective credit attribution is one of several very real concerns about LLMs that deserve to be taken seriously, and I also believe more effective web search stands to help. This is a huge topic of discussion amoung writers, artists, mathematicians and other creative careers. When the value a model delivers is a direct consequence of someone's creation, I think the future we all want to live in is one where that person is duly credited. This is extremely hard when facts are retrieved from the weights, but much more tractable when pages are cleanly retrieved and used in context. Even when facts are baked into the weights, it strikes me that the challenge of finding where in the training data it may have originated is a highly similar challenge to that of good retrieval from the web for highly rich queries. So my personal hope is that Exa can help us with that challenge.

I was pleased, then, to hear that this is something that people at Exa also think a lot about. One of them mentioned to me their efforts with [Exa Connect](https://exa.ai/connect) and partnering with traditional publications so they get paid for their original work.
