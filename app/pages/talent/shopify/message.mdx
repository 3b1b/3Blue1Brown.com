One of my favorite things is to delete code. So often the most stable solution to a problem looks like rethinking it from the ground up, and taking a smaller, more elegant approach rather than adding patches to a first attempt.

This is what made me smile so much in one part of a conversation I had with the Shopify team, where an engineer described presenting a new feature to their CEO, Tobi, who said, "If you were starting today, how would you do it?" The team mentioned a completely different approach, something simpler that they could only see with the clarity of hindsight, and Tobi said, "Great, do that". It didn't matter that the feature was supposed to ship the next day. The preference to do what was elegant and sustainable dominated. Every engineer is familiar with the feeling of only knowing what should be built after making a first attempt; that first attempt is not sunk cost, it's a necessary part of the process. But putting that into practice at scale is _so_ much easier said than done.

This also tracks very well with everything I know about Tobi, who is an engineer at heart, and the emphasis he places on elegance, which has disseminated into the full org. I once had a conversation with him where he observed that people with electrical engineering backgrounds often adapt more easily to management than those with pure software backgrounds. Perhaps it stems from a more deeply ingrained appreciation for minimalism and how best to operate under constraints.

Shopify also strikes me as wonderfully willing to try new and wild things. One year, recognizing the value of the internship program, their head of engineering decided to increase it not just a little bit, but to scale their plans from having 50 interns to 1000. I love that; if you have a strong opinion on something being worth doing, do it all the way.
