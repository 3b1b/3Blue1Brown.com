It's always a good sign when I sit down to chat with a company and they bring over a whiteboard. In this case, their team member Neil Chowdhury was excited to share with me their approach to eliciting [pathological behavior from LLMs](https://transluce.org/pathological-behaviors). How do you find out whether some set of prompts and context will elicit undesirable behavior from a model that otherwise seems generally well-trained and aligned? More specifically, how do you approach this as a systematic RL problem and not one of random prompt engineering?

Indeed, the approach was very pleasing and elegant (see the video). What struck me was how the flavor of the challenge he was tackling, fine-tuning a model when the rewards you're seeking are extremely sparse or even non-existent, is exactly the flavor of research you hear about from frontier model development itself. But usually all the talent best suited for those challenges flows to the labs themselves. Here, by contrast, you have a group of people who strongly believe that auditing these models should come from an independent third party.

This is just one example of the kind of research they do, but if this kind of work interests you, you can find much, much more on [their site](https://transluce.org/).
